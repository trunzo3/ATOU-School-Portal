## What we're building and why

A Touch of Understanding is a small nonprofit that runs disability-awareness workshops at schools. About 140 workshop days a year. Before each one, their program manager Pam has to email the school and collect logistics — how many students, which teachers, what time, which rooms. Right now she writes those emails one at a time and spends most of her week chasing replies.

We're replacing that with two things: a **school portal** where the school fills in the answers, and an **admin dashboard** where Pam sees everything at a glance. The answers flow into the Airtable base they already use, so nothing about their existing system changes.

Build it clean and simple. A school secretary should be able to open it on her phone between classes and finish in two minutes.

---

## The two ways into the app

There are exactly two, and nothing in between them.

**Schools** arrive on their own private link and land straight on their form. No menu, no welcome page, no choosing who they are.

**Pam** goes to the root address and lands on a login page for the admin dashboard.

The login page has an email field, a password field, and a sign-in button. Below that, visually separated, put a second button labelled **"Temporary: log in as Pam (development only)"** that signs her straight in with no credentials. It exists so we can build without account setup slowing us down, and it gets removed before this goes live.

Once signed in, a session keeps her signed in.

---

## The school portal

**How a school gets in.** Every school has its own link with a random, unguessable code in it, like `/s/8f3k9d2m`. Don't use the school name or a number that counts up — someone could guess their way into another school's information.

There's no password. When someone opens the link, they type their email address. We check it against the list of contacts for that school in Airtable. If their email is on that school's list, they're in. If it isn't, they don't get access — that's what stops a link forwarded to the wrong person from opening someone else's school.

They type their email every visit. It isn't remembered between visits. Several people share one link, and every change gets recorded under whoever made it, so we need to know who's there each time.

Schools often have several people on the list: a principal, an office admin, a few teachers. Any of them can open the link. They all see the same page and the same answers.

Pam's address, `programcoordinator@touchofunderstanding.org`, is authorized for every school and can edit even when a school is locked.

**Important:** contacts are never added inside our app. If a school has someone new, Pam adds them to the Contacts table in Airtable, and then they can get in.

The school's information pages are reachable from inside this portal. A school should never need a second link to find them.

## The four questions

Use this wording exactly. Pam has been sending these questions for years and they work.

### 1. Teachers' names for each grade level and student count for each teacher
*(this will allow us to pack the take-home materials separately for each teacher)*

This one needs a repeating block, not a text box. Each teacher gets a row with four fields:

- First name
- Last name
- Email address
- Student count

Start with one row showing. An "Add teacher" button adds another; each row has a remove button. Most schools will have two to five teachers.

All four fields are required. The student count especially — it determines how long the workshop day runs and what the school gets invoiced, so a row without it isn't usable.

Underneath the rows, show a running total of all the student counts. It updates live as they type. It's calculated, not typed — nobody can edit it directly.

As they fill in rows, save quietly in the background so nothing is lost if they wander off and come back.

### 2. Workshop time
*(Please provide a 3hr 15 minute time frame. This will allow for a break in between the workshop sections)*

Don't make them type a time range. Ask for one thing: the start time, chosen from a time picker. Store it as a real time value, not text — we'll be doing things with these times later.

From that start time, work out the rest and show it back for confirmation: 90 minutes, a 15-minute break, then another 90 minutes. A 8:00 AM start displays as "8:00 – 9:30 AM, break, 9:45 – 11:15 AM."

Underneath, add an optional note field for anything unusual about the timing. Some schools have a constraint the standard pattern can't express, and they should never be stuck.

### 3. Area for activity stations
*(2 classrooms near each other or MP room or Library)*

### 4. An additional separate area for speakers
*(if in a classroom, please provide #)*

### 5. Anything else we should know?

An open text box. Not required. Parking, gate codes, check-in procedure, a construction detour, a class that needs special consideration — whatever doesn't fit the questions above. It behaves like every other answer: it records who wrote it and when, and it keeps its history.

This one does not go to Airtable. It lives in our database and shows on Pam's screen.

## How answers get shown and changed

When someone opens the link, they see every question.

For a question already answered, show three things: the current answer, the email address of whoever entered it, and the date and time they did. Next to it, a change button and a field for a new answer.

Directly beneath the current answer, list every earlier answer to that same question — the old value itself, who entered it, and when. Show the two or three most recent inline and put anything older behind an expander.

This history is the point of the whole app. If a school said 75 students last week and someone changed it to 90 at 4pm the day before the workshop, Pam needs to see the 75, the person, and the hour — not a note saying the answer was edited twice. Store every save and show the values.

Every timestamp includes the time of day, not just the date.

The teacher block works the same way. Each save records the full teacher list as it stood at that moment, and those earlier versions display beneath the current rows.

Changing an existing answer pops a confirmation first, so it's never accidental. Log every save, including someone correcting their own entry a minute later.

Questions with no answer yet get a clear visual highlight so it's obvious what's still missing.

Somewhere visible, tell them plainly: answers can be changed any time before the workshop.

Everything saves as it's entered — there's no draft state that can be lost. At the bottom of the form, a "Done" button takes them to the confirmation page. It's a way of finishing and seeing what they've given you, not a way of submitting unsaved work.

---

## When they're done

Take them to a separate confirmation page that looks clearly different from the form — locked, not editable. It shows everything they've provided and highlights anything still missing. They can close the page knowing it saved.

Include a "Go back and adjust" link in case they want to keep going.

When someone returns later through the same link, they land on the form, not this page. They're coming back to change something.

## Printing a paper copy

Schools often gather this information on foot — the office admin walks to three classrooms and asks teachers for their counts before anyone sits down to type. Give them a print button on their form.

Printing produces a clean, purpose-built form, not a printout of the web page. Build it as its own layout: ATOU's name at the top, the school's name and workshop date, then each question laid out with room to write.

Where an answer already exists, it's printed in. Where one doesn't, there are ruled blank lines to fill in by hand — including several empty teacher rows with space for first name, last name, email, and count.

Someone should be able to carry that page around the school, write on it, come back, and type the answers in. Fit it on one page if it will, two at most.

Pam can print the same form from a school's page in her dashboard.

## The admin dashboard

This is Pam's home base. She should never need to open Airtable to do her job.

**The main view** is a grid. Schools down the left, the questions across the top. Where an answer is missing, the cell is flagged. Pam should be able to run her eye down one column and immediately see every school that hasn't given student counts yet. Partial answers show here as soon as they're entered — she doesn't wait for a submit.

The whole grid fits on screen without scrolling sideways. The school name is the first column and stays visible; it's the one she reads most.

**Each row** shows the school name, its workshop date, the state of each question, and a small copy icon that copies that school's private link for pasting into an email. Confirm visibly when it's copied.

**Clicking the school name opens that school's form** — the same form the school sees, with the same questions, the same editing, and the same history, rendered inside the admin dashboard. Pam is already recognized, so she is never asked to type an email to get in. Her edits record under her address exactly as a school's would, and schools see them the same way they see each other's.

Build the form once and use that same component in both places, so the two can never drift apart.

That school page also shows the school's authorized contacts, so Pam can see at a glance who is able to sign in, and the edit lock toggle for that school.

**Sorting and filtering.** Pam has about 140 workshops a year, so the grid needs to be workable at that size. She can sort by any column — school name, workshop date, or the state of any question. She can also filter to just the schools with something missing, which is the view she'll live in.

**Edit lock.** Answers stay open indefinitely; nothing locks on its own. Pam can lock or unlock any school at any time, and unlock it again afterward. Her own address can still edit a locked school.

**People who can sign in.** Give Pam a section where she can add other ATOU staff as admins — email and password — and remove them. Any admin can set another admin's password, which is how someone gets back in if they're locked out. Create Pam's own account by default, and seed a second admin account so nobody can strand themselves.

Self-service password reset over email comes later, once the email service is connected.

## Sending the email

Build the whole sending flow now, but don't connect an email service yet — we're adding Resend tomorrow. Set it up so that plugging in the service is the only remaining step.

Pam starts every send herself. Nothing fires automatically.

The app works out which schools are due by looking at the Workshop Date field in Airtable and finding the ones two months out. It shows Pam that list, and she sends.

- Sender address: `programcoordinator@touchofunderstanding.org`
- Subject line: `Logistics needed for A Touch of Understanding Workshop - School Name - Date of Workshop`
- Recipients come from the `Contact Email/s` field on the workshop record

Don't write the body copy. Pam writes it. Leave an editable email template in the dashboard with placeholder text and a note that it needs her wording, and make sure the school's link is inserted into whatever she writes.

Until Resend is connected, Pam copies each school's link from the dashboard and pastes it into her normal email. Schools can start using the portal right away.

## School information pages

Pam also needs a place to publish information for schools — program overviews, cost sheets, whatever changes year to year. Give her a page editor in the dashboard with real rich text: bold, underline, text size, color. She should never have to ask anyone to change a word of it.

Each page has a title, a web address, the body content, a sort order, and a published on/off switch. She can add pages, edit them, and unpublish the seasonal ones without deleting them.

Schools read these pages from inside their own portal.

Add a button that exports all pages, so ATOU has a copy outside the app.

Pages and any uploaded files live in the app, not in Airtable.

## Connecting to Airtable

They already have an Airtable base and it stays the system of record. Eventually we read from it and write to it. The details below describe how that will work, so build for it.

**Do not connect to Airtable in this build.** Don't ask anyone for an API key, and don't attempt any live reads or writes. Their base holds real workshop data for the school year and we're not risking it while the app is still new.

Instead, set up the connection so it's ready and leave it switched off. Put the API key, base identifier, and table identifier in settings, left empty for now. Build the read and write functions against those settings so that filling them in is the only step remaining.

In the meantime, run the app on realistic sample data: five made-up schools with contacts and workshop dates, one fully answered, a couple partly answered, and a couple untouched — and give at least one question a few rounds of edits by different people, so the change history has something real to display.

When we do connect, it will be to a copy of their base, not the live one.

Write answers back to these fields:

| What | Field ID |
|---|---|
| Teacher names with counts | `fldNxgfk2RObp4K1X` |
| Teacher email addresses | `fldaYxsKkM8PaIbzN` |
| Workshop time | `fld1O3lFuPbRypXtx` |
| Area for activity stations | `fldvc0hextGEnTZx3` |
| Additional area for speakers | `flduznKnoqFxXwbCB` |
| Total student count | `fldJp6j7NGxAy8zmk` |

Read these:

| What | Field ID |
|---|---|
| Workshop Date | `fldISmPGTYzFQjvn9` |
| Contact Email/s | `fldJVN4DX2Fq12VrY` |

Notes on the write-back:

- The total student count is the sum of the teacher rows. Nobody types it.
- Teacher names and emails go into single text fields, with **one teacher per line**. Names format as `First Last: 24`. Emails go in the same order, one per line. This matches how Pam's table already looks.
- Latest answer wins. Overwrite the field — Airtable holds the current state, our app holds the history.
- Contact Email/s is a lookup field, so it's read-only. Don't try to write to it.

**Information flows one direction after the first read.** We read Airtable to know which schools are due and who to email, and we write answers back. We never read answers back out of Airtable to fill in the form. Our app is where answers get changed.

Keep the detailed data in our own database: each teacher as a separate record with their own name, email, and count, plus the full history of every change. The single-line text going into Airtable is just formatting so it fits the field Pam already has.

## How it should look and behave

Clean and plain. A school secretary opening this between classes should understand it instantly — generous text, obvious buttons, no clutter, no jargon.

It has to work well on a phone. Some of these people will fill it in standing in a hallway.

It also has to be accessible: full keyboard navigation, works with screen readers, real labels on every field, good color contrast. Never use color alone to show that something's missing — pair it with text or an icon. This organization exists to serve people with disabilities and their own website is built for accessibility. Ours has to be too.

Store any uploaded files in Replit App Storage, not the local filesystem, so they survive redeployment.

Use Postgres for the app's own data — schools, answers, full change history, teacher rows, info pages, and admin accounts.

Store the app's own web address as an environment variable rather than writing it into the code. School links are built from it, and ATOU will move to their own domain later. Links already sitting in schools' inboxes need to keep working when that happens.

Times and timestamps are Pacific. ATOU and every school they serve are in California.
